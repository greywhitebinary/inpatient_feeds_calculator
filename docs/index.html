Feed. Form. Flow.

Nutrition support tools and writing, by a dietitian.

Two free calculators for enteral nutrition, and a newsletter about the thinking behind them. Both tools show their working, cite where their numbers came from, and leave the clinical decision with the clinician.

Built by Hui Jun Chew, Dietitian.

The tools

ENCalc

Plan a new tube feed or review one already running for an adult inpatient. ENCalc brings the feed, modulars, oral nutritional supplements, IV fluids, propofol, water and flushes into one daily view, using Canadian manufacturer product information and a formulary you can edit for your site.

BTFCalc

Estimate the energy, protein, fluid and micronutrients in a home-blended tube feed, then see how changes to the blend or the rest of the day affect the total. BTFCalc uses the Canadian Nutrient File as its food reference data and asks for the measured final volume, because ingredient weights cannot reliably predict what a blend comes to.

Why these exist

Most nutrition calculators available online were built for a different health system. They use American products, American reference data and American reporting conventions, so a Canadian dietitian gets an answer built on a formulary they cannot order from.

Both tools are built the other way round. Product values come from Canadian manufacturer documents, each record carries the source document, page and the date it was checked, and values stay editable because local labels and institutional formularies differ.

They also try to be honest about what is not known. Where a manufacturer has not disclosed a value, the tools show an em dash rather than a zero, because an undisclosed figure and a measured zero are different facts and merging them misstates the label.

Neither tool sets a target, recommends a plan or assesses an individual. They do arithmetic and show their working, so the clinician can see what each input changed.

The writing

Feed. Form. Flow. is a newsletter about nutrition support practice, and about the decisions behind these tools — how water is accounted for, how product data is sourced and verified, and what a calculator should and should not decide on a clinician’s behalf.

These tools are for dietitians and teams supporting nutrition support. They support, but do not replace, clinical judgement.
